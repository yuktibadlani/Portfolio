Case study 1: AI search (AEO/GEO)

From 300 to 12.6K+ AI citations in four months

The challenge
AI Overviews and AI Mode had become the first screen for news queries, but our answers were buried in editorial language and rarely cited. Tracking in Semrush gave a baseline of about 300 citations.
What I did
Proposed and led a shift to answerable content: direct answers up front, written plainly, backed by a site-wide schema overhaul. I piloted it on a small set of evergreen pages, then used the early gains to bring Editorial and content heads on board.
The result
Citations grew from 300 to more than 12.6K across ChatGPT, Gemini, AI Mode, and AI Overviews, with a clear rise in impressions. Player profiles, stats, and evergreen pages led the way, and the approach became the site-wide content playbook.

Case study 2: Web architecture

Rebuilding the URL architecture of a site with hundreds of thousands of pages

The challenge
A 2019 structure built without SEO input: no sitemap, URLs five or more levels deep with no hub pages, and IDs that changed while still returning 200, creating duplicates with no canonicals.

One scorecard URL, before and after

Before/scorecard/id/id/id/id/slug/id/tab-name After/cricket-live-score/slug/tab-name
What I did
Rebuilt the architecture around real hub pages and shallow URLs. Rolled it out in phases with 301s, starting with the highest-authority section, added per-category sitemaps and canonicals, and launched in the off-season with Engineering and Product.
The result
A two-week ranking dip, then gains for top keyword groups. Indexing returned to seconds after a brief rise to 30 minutes, and crawling settled at a healthy 85/15 refresh-to-discovery balance.

Case study 3: Site migrations

Unlocking Google Discover with a news-module migration

The challenge
Discover was barely used as a channel. Images were below Discover’s size requirements, structured data had problems, and much of the content didn’t match what Discover’s guidelines reward.
What I did
Merged about 5,000 news URLs into the restructured site, fixed image sizes and rebuilt schema site-wide at template level, and reorganised titles, images, and topics around clusters, using curiosity-building but honest titles learned from Open Graph tests.
The result
After a temporary 20% dip, organic traffic rose sharply and held. Discover began climbing 20 days after the merge, growing from 2.3K to 4.2M active users during IPL season, with CTR up from 0.66% to 7.57%.

Case study 4: Algorithm recovery

Recovering from a 60% core update drop in under 60 days

The challenge
The June 2025 Core Update cut 60% of visibility overnight, right as a busy stretch of the season began. Losses were concentrated in templated analysis and prediction pages, which fit Google’s tightened stance on scaled content.
What I did
Ran technical and content audits, then isolated low-value templated pages through high-impression, low-CTR patterns in Search Console. I consolidated the strongest, retired the rest with 410s in batches across 8,000+ URLs, and set new content-quality guidelines, making the case to Editorial despite pushback.
The result
Visibility began rising after 25–30 days and reached pre-drop levels within 50–60 days, during a quiet part of the sporting calendar with no seasonal surge to help.
